### 📈 AI Trends

**1. An Anthropic mathematician claims he and Claude proved the Riemann hypothesis — nobody has verified it yet**
🏷️ Source: ai.info / The Verge / Scientific American; Alpöge's own note (alpo.ge/remark.pdf)
📝 Core: Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, posted a four-page note on his own site claiming that he and Claude found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, formalized in Lean, and said the method extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. The proof has not been independently verified, and Anthropic has neither commented nor taken part in the announcement.
💡 Why it matters: This is the 1859 conjecture behind a $1M Clay Millennium Prize. Claude's own August attempt did not prove it — it raised the known lower bound on the share of zeros on the critical line from 41.6% to 67.2%, using 31 million output tokens and roughly 60 subagents. If this holds, it is the first time an AI has been a named party to a Millennium Problem solution; but the rules require publication, at least two years of scrutiny and general acceptance by the mathematics community before the prize is even considered. The interesting shift is not "AI solved it" — it is that the verification burden has moved onto human institutions.

**2. Thinking Machines open-sources Inkling 2 Mini: looped layers put a 26.4B model on a 32GB laptop**
🏷️ Source: Thinking Machines Lab / The Decoder
📝 Core: Mira Murati's Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini, a 26.4B-parameter mixture-of-experts model in which 12 of its 24 layers run three times per token, for 5.4B effective active parameters — Apache 2.0, a 1M-token context window, and small enough to run locally on a 32GB MacBook Pro or an Nvidia DGX Spark.
💡 Why it matters: Looping lets the model think longer without emitting more text, which the company says finds answers with fewer tokens. It leads all open models under 100B on agentic coding: 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1, 77.9% on SWE-bench Pro v2 Hard, 46.5% on Vals Index v2.2. Artificial Analysis puts it at 20 on its Intelligence Index — tied with Qwen3.8 27B at roughly a sixth of the cost and four times the speed. Open weights plus laptop deployment narrows the gap between frontier capability and capability that runs outside any API guardrail.

Underwriters then exercised the full greenshoe, buying 147 million more shares at the offer price for another $12.2B and pushing total proceeds to $93.5B, past SpaceX's June record.
💡 Why it matters: A concurrent private placement brought $26.0B from AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC Partners, and Anthropic estimates net proceeds near $104.8B. ### 🛡️ AI Safety

**1. METR: models now match skilled humans on verifiable work, horizons double every 3.6 months — and the measurement is getting harder**
🏷️ Source: METR (Q4 2026 capability update, 20 Nov)
📝 Core: METR could not find human baseliners who outperform the current frontier models on its coding and optimization tasks; across the combined TH1.1 and TH2 suites, 50% time horizons are doubling roughly every 3.6 months. METR states that current public evidence is insufficient to rule out drastic AI R&D acceleration within the next couple of years.
💡 Why it matters: The sharpest finding is about measurability. On tasks where the true quality of a solution is hard to determine — slow feedback, weak tests of generalization — agents show worse judgment than human experts, and model judges overestimate outputs produced by models as strong as or stronger than themselves. Agent swarms helped only on verifiable tasks with clear metrics and fast feedback, and had unclear or negative effects elsewhere. And "not one" AI-designed evaluation was judged useful by METR's researchers, with Opus 5.5 only borderline above the bar — the most concrete counter-evidence yet to the "AI accelerates AI R&D" story.

**2. OpenAI: adversarial-distillation defenses cut reasoning-trace extraction from 54% to 0.2%**
🏷️ Source: OpenAI (Combatting adversarial distillation)
📝 Core: OpenAI detailed how it detects and disrupts adversarial distillation — using a frontier model's outputs without authorization to train another model, then deploying it without comparable safeguards. In an internal adaptive red-team evaluation, the average attack success rate at recovering GPT-6.2 Astra's protected reasoning fell from 54.0% against GPT-6 Astra with its original safeguards to 0.2%.
💡 Why it matters: The load-bearing result is that exact chain-of-thought traces transfer more capability than reasoning summaries. Starting from the same pretrained student, the run trained on exact traces beat the summary-trained run on cyber benchmarks after both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. That gives a concrete safety rationale for showing users summaries while keeping full traces server-side. Defenses work at two levels: request-level classifiers, model behavior and API controls, plus campaign-level detection of coordinated extraction across accounts.

**3. UN killer-robot talks end with 128 states agreeing only to "elaborate" a possible accord by 2028**
🏷️ Source: Future of Life Institute / UN Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons, 7th Review Conference
📝 Core: The CCW's seventh Review Conference in Geneva ended early Saturday with 128 states party agreeing only to "elaborate" a possible instrument by 2028, rather than launching binding ban negotiations backed by nearly 100 countries. The United States, Israel, Belarus and Britain led opposition to a specific mandate.
💡 Why it matters: FLI co-founder Max Tegmark called it "an empty agreement" produced by the CCW's consensus rule, which lets a minority veto the majority, and urged frustrated states to "take the issue elsewhere, to an assembly where their voices will be heard." His framing is that this is the 14th consecutive year of talking without acting while the weapons proliferate. Autonomous-weapons risk has therefore moved from a future governance problem to a present one whose governance mechanism has demonstrably failed.

### Today's takeaway

All three trend stories share one gap: claims are outrunning verification. A four-page Riemann note, Anthropic's claim last week that Claude solved two open black-hole problems, and Architect Labs' pre-silicon claim that an AI-designed CPU (Frostbite) reaches 4.59x the performance per watt of a conventional core across 25 workloads all await outside checking — on the same day METR says measurement itself is getting harder. Capital moved the other way: a record $93.5B IPO plus $26B of strategic placement says the market will pay for unverified capability. Governance stalled again at the CCW, while industry partially filled the gap — Vals AI launched a public misalignment-incident tracker, and TuringSpace measured a real gap between models (74.1%) and humans (94.1%) on interactive spatial puzzles. Capability and capital are advancing; verification and governance are not. That asymmetry is the safety story of the year.
