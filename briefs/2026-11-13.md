**2. Edge0 open-sources a 145B on-device model that runs at 80 tokens/s on a laptop**
🏷️ Source: Edge0 (2026-11-12)
📝 One-line core: Edge0-145B and Edge0-120B are out, with the larger model activating roughly 10B of its 145B parameters per step and reporting 29GB peak memory and 80+ tokens/s decoding on a laptop; it scores 86.52 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (DeepSeek-V4-Flash 82.7, Claude 78.2), 90.4 on GPQA-Diamond and 80.6 on IFBench, where Claude Opus 4.6 gets 62.5.

That sidesteps the memory wall that kept on-device models under 10B parameters, and it moves faster than cloud price cuts toward a genuinely usable local frontier model for data that cannot leave the building.

### 🛡️ AI Safety

**1. Four nonprofits sue five labs to freeze frontier model training**
🏷️ Source: mlq.ai (citing Public Citizen, CBS News, Al Jazeera)
📝 One-line core: The Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and the Alliance for Secure AI sued Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceX's AI unit in San Francisco Superior Court on Nov 12, alleging violations of California's Unfair Competition Law plus public nuisance, and asking for an emergency injunction freezing training and deployment of next-generation models until the companies can prove the systems are under human control.

**2. FBI discloses FaceStealer, a North Korean deepfake campaign that stole over $400M**
🏷️ Source: Nextgov (citing FBI / DOJ, 2026-11-12)
📝 One-line core: Operation FaceStealer uses secretly recorded webcam video and AI-generated deepfakes to impersonate contacts in fake Zoom and Teams meetings, then pushes targets to install malware disguised as an audio fix; it netted more than $400M in crypto over 18 months, and the FBI has identified 677 targets in 58 countries and seized 900-plus victim webcam videos plus cloud infrastructure.
💡 Why it matters: The entry point is trust, not a software flaw — invitations arrive from genuine Telegram, LinkedIn, Slack and Discord accounts the attackers already control, and access to old conversations makes the messages sound native. Earlier this year a JavaScript library maintainer joined a fake Teams meeting featuring someone they recognised and followed instructions that infected their machine, wiring supply-chain risk into the open-source ecosystem. The FBI's Jeremy Robertson calls the last six months a significant evolution in scale and sophistication; the defence is unglamorous — verify meeting requests out of band.

**3. Podqi study finds shopping chatbots send users to risky sites up to 15% of the time**
🏷️ Source: StartupFortune (citing Podqi, 2026-11-13)
📝 One-line core: Across 6,795 AI shopping answers covering 151 brands, Perplexity recommended risky or counterfeit retailers in 15% of responses, ChatGPT in 8% and Gemini in 4%; risky sites were suggested 6.3 times as often as they were warned against, and ChatGPT flagged a risky listing as unreliable in only 24% of the cases where it surfaced one.
💡 Why it matters: This is the first scaled audit of agentic commerce, and it converts hallucination into payable loss — in 57% of answers containing risky sites the chatbot actively recommended buying. Smaller brands saw roughly double the exposure of large ones, so the harm is unevenly distributed: the merchants least able to afford brand protection lose the traffic. For anyone handing procurement or checkout to an agent, retrieval quality now belongs on the pre-launch acceptance list.

### Today's takeaway

All three trend stories point the same way: capability is sinking into smaller units — Hydrogen makes long-context agents cheap with flat pricing, Edge0 fits 145B parameters into a laptop, and Nscale's filing shows the heavy-asset model underneath it all is still burning cash. Safety is being squeezed from both directions. On one side, four nonprofits are trying to use unfair-competition law to stop training outright; on the other, the industry is productising safety — OpenAI used Thursday's Cyber at the Frontier keynote to launch Daybreak Red Team and Defense Factory, the latter built on GPT-6 Cyber and able to open merge-ready patch PRs, alongside a $1B defenders subsidy, while IBM's Granite 4.1 LLMON cut indirect prompt-injection success from 53–100% to 0.02–5.6% and cautioned the behaviour is learned, not enforced. The FBI case and the Podqi audit are the reminder that the incidents landing first are rarely about a model being too clever: they are about impersonated trust and poisoned retrieval. The cheaper and more local capability gets, the lower the unit cost of that kind of harm.
