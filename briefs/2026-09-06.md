### 🛡️ AI Safety

📌 **Sanders & Casar bill would ban "artificial superintelligence" — up to 20 years in prison**
🏷️ Source: Sanders Senate press release (Sep 3) · Washington Post / Computer Weekly (Sep 3–4) · LessWrong debate (Sep 6)
📝 The "Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act" from Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Greg Casar would permanently ban developing or deploying superintelligent AI, and pause advanced AI development until a new cabinet-level federal agency sets safety rules and a model-review process. Violators face the "corporate death penalty" (dissolution) and individuals up to 20 years in prison — modeled on penalties for unlawful nuclear-weapons development — plus a push for international agreements and export controls. It directly responds to this summer's rogue-agent incidents (Hugging Face, the German wiki); LessWrong is now debating pause vs. slowdown and enforceable-pause mechanisms.
💡 Significance: AI governance is crossing from voluntary lab commitments into criminal legislation — Congress is treating agent misalignment as a national-security problem.

🏷️ Source: LessWrong — "Review of the CB risk determination in the Claude Mythos 5.1 System Card" (MCNAIR, Sep 5)
📝 Independent assessor MCNAIR agrees with Anthropic that Mythos 5.1 likely does not cross the CB-2 (chemical/biological uplift) threshold, but flags: the determination leans on human-intensive evals with fewer than ten experts — only three graded chemical-weapons uplift; the automated eval may under-elicit the model (models got an estimated 2–10x fewer resources than humans); no new CB-2 automated evals since May 28; and no third-party pre-assessment of CB risk was offered (unlike autonomy risks). 💡 Significance: A rare public third-party audit of a system card — and a warning that subjective, tiny-sample, time-intensive evals cannot keep pace with accelerating model capabilities.
