**📌 IBM 用 LLMON 把間接提示注入成功率壓到 5.6% 以下**

**📌 模擬環境裡的 probe 監控器，準度幾乎和真實環境一樣**
🏷️ 來源：LessWrong（控制評估研究）
📝 一項控制評估研究讓 Qwen3.5-27B 對 LinuxArena 的 1,313 個任務發動攻擊，真實執行兩次、模擬執行一次，再用六個 activation probe 在每指令 1% 誤報率下偵測惡意行為。結果模擬環境的平均攔截率只比真實環境低 1.0 個百分點，95% 區間為 −3.0 到 +1.0；作為對照，兩次真實執行彼此的差距就有 0.2 個百分點。研究團隊另外訓練出能區分模擬與真實 activation 的分類器，證明資料確實不同，但這個落差沒有實質吃掉監控器效能。
💡 意義：安全評估長期被迫在模擬裡做，因為不能真的把可能失準的 agent 放上生產系統，而模擬一直被質疑「太乾淨、測不出東西」。這篇給出第一個量化答案：白盒監控器（probe）在模擬中的表現可以外推到真實部署，讓控制評估的結論至少對這一類防禦成立。

### 📈 AI Trends

**📌 OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet so agents can pay on a user's behalf**
🏷️ Source: OpenAI blog
📝 OpenAI rolled out ChatGPT Wallet on November 13 with American Express, Mastercard and Visa. A user adds a card once; before each purchase they review the items, total and selected card, then approve with Face ID or a fingerprint scan. The agent receives a payment token issued by the network, so it never sees raw card details, and existing card rewards and dispute protections still apply. Availability starts with US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and over, through the Dots agents, and OpenAI says it is not taking a cut of transactions at launch.
💡 Why it matters: This is the last mile of agent commerce. Agents could already compare, fill forms and build carts, but they could not pay. Now there is a payment rail merchants can accept with their existing systems. The deliberately small launch and the zero take rate are about buying compatibility data from merchants, acquirers and card networks — whoever lays this rail first defines the default etiquette for agent payments.

**📌 Sierra ships Poppy 1.0, and every Shopify store auto-publishes it**
🏷️ Source: Sierra / AI Weekly
📝 Sierra released Personal Agent Protocol — Poppy — 1.0 on November 13, defining how a user's personal agent discovers a company and negotiates which actions it is allowed to take, through APIs, websites or the company's own agent. The distribution is the story: every Shopify store in more than 175 countries now auto-publishes a Poppy file with no merchant setup, Meta's Muse agent looks for Poppy on every site it visits by default, and 60 additional design partners including Cisco, Delta, Intuit, Twilio and Zoom pushed adoption past 100 companies.
💡 Why it matters: The agent economy was never short on models; it was short on handshakes. Poppy turns "my agent acts for me" from a demo into a default pipeline, and moves what an agent may read and do into a standard file instead of leaving it to scrapers and guesswork. Landing in the same week as ChatGPT Wallet means agents got a payment rail and a passport at once.

### 🛡️ AI Safety

**📌 FBI warns ARTEX autonomous pentest framework drives sub-3-hour breaches**
🏷️ Source: FBI advisory FLASH-20261113-001 (via GBHackers)
📝 The FBI issued FLASH-20261113-001 on November 13 warning that threat actors are weaponizing ARTEX, a penetration-testing framework written in Go that converts natural-language objectives into machine commands executed by specialized agents. It ships no embedded model and instead connects to external services including Claude, GPT, DeepSeek, GLM, Grok and Kimi. Some intrusions progressed from initial targeting to privileged access in under three hours. Reported campaigns stole personal data on roughly 67,000 individuals in South Korea and compromised a ConnectWise Cloud Backup tenant reaching more than 2,250 downstream customers. Its developer, "Autumn-27," released it in July and pulled the GitHub repository on October 8 after abuse reports, but forks and ARTEX PRO persist; researchers found over 1,600 publicly exposed consoles by early November.
💡 Why it matters: This is the first agentic attack campaign with an official advisory number and published indicators. The defensive window has compressed from days to hours. The advisory also notes that Chinese models, DeepSeek Flash in particular, predominate in attacker-controlled deployments for cost, capability and "limited observable guardrails" — model choice has become part of the attack surface.

**📌 IBM's LLMON pushes indirect prompt-injection success below 6%**
🏷️ Source: IBM Granite / Hugging Face blog
📝 IBM released Granite 4.1 3B and 8B LLMON-preview models on November 12, replacing conventional chat templates with LLMON, an LLM-native markup language that wraps every context block — system prompt, instructions, data, documents, tool definitions and tool results — in semantically meaningful tags inside reserved special-token delimiters, and tags each block with an execution permission. In internal tests, indirect prompt-injection success fell from 53–100% on baselines to 0.02–5.6% once the privilege rule treated only exec-authoritative blocks as commands. A GitHub toolkit and templates ship alongside.
💡 Why it matters: This upgrades "separate instructions from data" from a prompting trick into model structure. Previously the defence was asking the model to ignore instructions embedded in a document; now the data structurally lacks the standing to be an instruction. IBM is careful about the limit: the behaviour is learned, not enforced, so it should not be the sole defence for agents with access to sensitive systems.

**📌 Probe monitors hold up in simulated control environments**
🏷️ Source: LessWrong (control evaluation research)
📝 A control-evaluation study ran Qwen3.5-27B attacks against 1,313 LinuxArena tasks — twice for real and once in simulation — then tested six activation probes at a 1% per-command false-positive rate. On average across probes, the attack catch rate in simulation was only 1.0 percentage points lower than in real runs, with a 95% interval of −3.0 to +1.0 points. For comparison, two real runs differ by 0.2 points. The team also trained a probe that successfully distinguished simulated from real activations, confirming the data is measurably different — yet that difference did not materially degrade monitor performance.
💡 Why it matters: Safety evaluations are often forced into simulation, because you cannot put a possibly-misaligned agent on production systems, and simulation has always been suspect for being too clean to test anything. This gives a first quantitative answer: for white-box probe monitors, simulated control evaluations appear to extrapolate to real deployment.

### Today's takeaway

The through-line today is that agents now have their own settlement layer — and their own attack surface. On the trends side, ChatGPT Wallet gave agents a payment rail and Poppy 1.0 gave them a handshake protocol, both landing in the same week, moving agents from "can do things" to "can transact." On the safety side the news is symmetrical: the FBI's ARTEX advisory shows the same multi-agent orchestration, pointed the other way, compressing reconnaissance-to-privilege into under three hours, with model choice (DeepSeek Flash) itself a cost-and-guardrail trade-off.

Two infrastructure signals are worth noting. DeepSeek exposed five app-only tools — search, file input, URL reading, safety checks and speech — as standalone APIs, priced at $0.75 per 1,000 search or URL requests and $0.225 per 1,000 OCR pages. And llama.cpp shipped v1.0.0 after three and a half years, committing to semantic versioning for breaking C API changes, which is what turns local inference from "it runs" into "you can depend on it."

Macro: the Federal Reserve's semi-annual Financial Stability Report named AI the top risk to the financial system, cited by 87% of market contacts versus 50% in the spring, with public debt tied to advanced computing now above $1.1T. JPMorgan simultaneously lifted its 2027 US investment-grade supply forecast to a record $2.1T, with $612B of net new tech and media issuance. The most optimistic commercial story and the most sober systemic-risk assessment in this brief are, in other words, pointing at the same money.
