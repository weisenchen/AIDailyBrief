**2. 📌 Anthropic says Claude Fable 5.2 solved two open problems in black hole physics**
🏷️ Source: Anthropic Research / AI Weekly
📝 Anthropic's research post says Claude completed the calculation physicists laid out in 2003 for the 1983 Hawking–Page black-hole phase transition — a computation the post calls "extremely complex" and never finished — and found the spectral curve governing the modular bootstrap at large c, proving the lightest black hole cannot exceed c/Λ with Λ ≈ 9.1266. An earlier Claude had failed the second problem across 61 parallel attempts. The work ran as a researcher's side project: copies of the model worked as a small research group for about six weeks, nearly every step computed twice independently, results formalised in Lean in September, the paper written by Claude and every main result checked by a physicist.
💡 Anthropic draws the boundary itself: neither result describes our universe, neither has practical applications, and neither comes close to settling quantum gravity. The same release's "1,000 agents formalised Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours" is a conversion of Wiles's existing proof, not new mathematics. The open question is procedural — AI-generated physics is now circulating faster than expert audit, and no independent physicist assessment has been identified.

**2. 📌 TwinLab-Bench caps the best model at 45/100 on autonomous experiment design**
🏷️ Source: arXiv 2611.26248 / AI Weekly
📝 The preprint pairs each of 52 physical-science, life-science and engineering tasks with its own "TwinLab agent" that reads a real lab's raw material, calls calculators built from its records and returns readings for whatever experiment the model designs. Across 13 agents, Claude Fable 5.2 leads at 45.0, Opus 5.5 reaches 40.2 and GPT-6.1-sol 31.7, with DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi and GLM lower; expert human routes score 97.8 when replayed.
💡 The benchmark targets the hardest of three abilities — not running a workflow or picking the next measurement, but designing the experiment. Authors Yang Wan and Linchao Zhu find the tasks "remain hard for frontier models"; the commonest failure is accepting a mechanism without discriminating tests (7 of Opus 5.5's 16 failures), and doubling the compute budget rescues only 2 of 21 failed runs. It is a useful counterweight to the week's "AI scientist" narrative.

**3. 📌 Five major US banks now put AI-agent hack losses on the customer**
🏷️ Source: TechCrunch / AI Weekly
📝 Citi, PNC and Citizens updated their terms on November 18 to hold customers responsible for anything third-party AI agents do in their accounts — including activity after credentials leak or the agent itself is hacked. 💡 This turns agentic risk from a technical problem into a contract problem: hallucinated actions, bad tool calls and stolen sessions all land on the end user. For enterprises wiring agents into purchasing, payments and support, it is a more direct cost signal than any benchmark; for regulators, it is ready evidence that liability allocation has not kept pace with deployment.
