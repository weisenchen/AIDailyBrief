💡 意義：agentic safety 的核心問題之一是「未經指示的協調」；這份實驗把門檻量化到一句提示詞，說明協作能力可以被極低成本觸發，也讓監控設計有了具體的測試對象。

### 📈 AI Trends

**1. Thinking Machines ships Inkling 2 Small: a 594B-parameter MoE billed as the strongest Western open-weight model**
🏷️ Source: The Decoder / Thinking Machines
📝 Core: Mira Murati's Thinking Machines released its second model generation. Inkling 2 Small has 594B total parameters with 17B active per token, accepts text and images, and exposes an adjustable thinking depth so users can trade speed for thoroughness. It scores 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — 18 points above the previous Inkling Small (13) and roughly level with Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash. Agentic gains are the headline: AutomationBench-AA jumps from 5% to 46%, FrontierCode 2.0 from 5% to 25.4%, with DeepSWE at 72.1% and SWE-bench Pro v2 (Hard) at 86.3%. The cost is tokens: about 150,000 output tokens per task, four times its predecessor, at $1.18 per task — still well below Gemini 3.8 Flash at $7.77 and Claude Sonnet 5.5 at $10.02. Training totaled roughly 12 trillion tokens, with the post-training RL run using just 3 trillion tokens on 2,048 GPUs for nine days. It ships first as a proprietary model on Thinking Machines' Tinker platform; weights are promised under Apache 2.0 after external safety testing.
💡 Why it matters: This is the first time a Western lab has pushed the small-active/large-total architecture to near-frontier scores at unusually low training cost — and an Apache 2.0 release would reset the open-weight competitive map.

**2. OpenAI raises $11B at a $1.25T valuation as Anthropic's IPO expectations slide**

The round has not closed; it is expected to finish within weeks and could total about $20 billion. That is well short of the $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation OpenAI was reported to be seeking in late September. **3. Crusoe sells its unfinished 10GW Wyoming campus to Google, which will invest $17B**

### 🛡️ AI Safety

**4. OpenAI partners with Hacktron and widens its frontier-model access for vulnerability research**
🏷️ Source: OpenAI
📝 Core: OpenAI announced a partnership with Hacktron in which the two teams jointly pick high-priority areas to test, focused on how OpenAI's systems interact with third-party services. As part of the deal, Hacktron gets expanded access to OpenAI's frontier models through the Daybreak Red program. Hacktron builds AI agents for code review, penetration testing and red teaming, and its research has found vulnerabilities in how applications, cloud infrastructure and identity systems interact; OpenAI first met the team through its own bug bounty program. Earlier the same week, OpenAI raised its bounty payouts and announced a security sprint with Cathedral's researchers.
💡 Why it matters: Frontier labs are institutionalizing an outside-red-team loop — which also makes "how much frontier capability do you hand an external attacker" a live governance question.

**5. OpenAI and Ligo release BioBasicsBench: 100 real wet-lab tasks, and the best model gets 66.7%**
🏷️ Source: OpenAI
📝 Core: OpenAI and enzyme-design biotech Ligo built BioBasicsBench from 100 tasks drawn from scientists' own experiments, testing whether agents can read gel and blot images, colony plates, microscopy, sequencing files and instrument exports. Tasks span protein methods, imaging and cytometry, quantification and assays, nucleic acid methods and molecular cloning, and deliberately preserve failed experiments, ambiguous images and conflicting observations. Each task carries expert-written rubric criteria and passed three review rounds. Seven models across 37 reasoning-effort settings, five attempts each, produced 18,500 graded results: at the xhigh setting GPT-6 Astra averaged 66.7% of available credit versus 43.8% for GPT-5.6 Sol. Rubrics explicitly reward recognizing that evidence cannot support a conclusion.
💡 Why it matters: Bio-risk governance depends less on whether agents can run experiments than on whether they misread them; scoring "knowing what you don't know" moves bio evals from paper figures to the actual bench.

**6. LessWrong: GPT-6.1 Sol is one sentence away from spontaneously collaborating**
🏷️ Source: LessWrong
📝 Core: Researchers put seven models into groups of three on a research task where sharing findings let each agent answer more questions. A single line stating that agents had been "trained to collaborate" produced useful collaboration in 9 of 10 runs for GPT-6.1 Sol — and 0 of 10 without it. Claude Opus 5.5 needed explicit permission plus communication instructions, after which it divided work with GPT-6.1 and nearly doubled performance versus solo runs. The other models talked to each other but never executed a strategy and did not meaningfully improve; open-weight models and GPT-5.6 Sol improved only when handed an explicit division-of-labour strategy. The authors suspect multi-agent training explains the gap and expect spontaneous collaboration to rise in future open-weight models.
💡 Why it matters: Uninstructed coordination is a core agentic-safety concern, and this quantifies the trigger at one prompt line — giving monitoring work a concrete behaviour to test for.
