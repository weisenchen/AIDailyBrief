**2. Google DeepMind ships AlphaGenome 2, nearly doubling causal-variant detection**
🏷️ Source: Google DeepMind (via AI Weekly / BigGo)
📝 One-line core: The new DNA sequence model was trained on more than 107 million individual cell profiles across 68 tissues; on the TenK10K dataset it detects 30% of causal variants versus 16% for AlphaGenome 1, and gets the direction of effect right 43% of the time versus 23%.
💡 Why it matters: This is cell-type-specific prediction of variant effect rather than coarse genome-wide correlation, which is a real step for rare-disease locus interpretation and drug-target triage. It is available today through the AlphaGenome API for non-commercial research, with commercial research in private preview and AlphaGenome 2 Skills coming to Google Antigravity.

**3. Huawei open-sources MACaQ, quantizing a 122B model in 64 minutes**
🏷️ Source: Huawei Cloud & Smart Lab (via AI Weekly)
📝 One-line core: The Apache 2.0 framework covers text, image, video and audio models across llama.cpp, vLLM, Diffusers and PyTorch, and the company claims a 122B-parameter model can be quantized in 64 minutes on 32.5GB of device memory, with 2.33x / 2.58x prefill and decode speedups for Qwen3.8-27B under W4A4.
💡 Why it matters: Quantization used to require a GPU cluster; MACaQ pulls it down to single-machine memory, which lowers the bar for deploying large models on hardware Chinese labs can actually buy. Companion GGUFs including Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MACaQ-R shipped the same day — the technical substrate under this week's shift of enterprise procurement toward open-weight models to cap bills.

🏷️ Source: OpenAI
📝 One-line core: At the Paris Peace Forum, OpenAI signed the Charter of Engagement for Safer AI for Children and committed $2 million to the scientific research network under the Coalition for Children's Rights and Protection in the Age of AI.
💡 Why it matters: The money is earmarked for shared measurement tools — instruments researchers can use to quantify AI's risks and opportunities for children — rather than another statement of principles. **3. Radical Numerics ships Omnii Defense: offline pathogen detection on a laptop and a USB sequencer**
🏷️ Source: Radical Numerics / Cryptobriefing
📝 One-line core: Built on the Omnii-1 genome language model, the edge version runs offline on a laptop plus a USB gene sequencer and posts 95.8% pathogen recall at a 0.5 threshold against 78.6% for DIAMOND on the same measure, with trials under way at Lawrence Livermore and other national labs.
💡 Why it matters: Biosecurity defence has always been bottlenecked by the absence of cloud connectivity in the field, so offline deployment pushes detection to borders, field sites and labs. But the company's benchmark compares against a single tool, and recall is only half the story — false-alarm rate decides whether anyone keeps using it, which is why access is limited to vetted testers while evaluation continues.
