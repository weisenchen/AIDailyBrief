### 🛡️ AI Safety

**4. HCLTech paper: a frontier model can directly rewrite another model's weights**
🏷️ Source: alphaXiv — *Weight Space Misfolding* (submitted 2026-11-14)
📝 In one line: Researchers had GPT-6.1-Sol act as an editor that directly modified weights inside Qwen2.5-1.5B; a factual-substitution edit succeeded on all 100 tests while damaging the target's ability to copy the original word normally.
💡 Why it matters: The authors call this "weight space misfolding," by analogy to prion disease, and note the asymmetry — an attacker only needs to succeed once, while a defender must find that needle across the entire weight space. Refusal-language and name-suppression edits were unreliable. The paper states plainly that it does not demonstrate self-propagation or actual offensive capability, and that no public model can yet do this reliably — but the underlying capability moved from GPT-5.6-Sol to 6.1-Sol in five months. Their recommendation is patch inspection, provenance tracking and rollback rather than banning weight editing outright.

**5. A stolen Fireworks key got a backdoored Claude Code extension onto Cursor's marketplace**
🏷️ Source: BleepingComputer, Microsoft Security Blog
📝 In one line: Cursor confirmed an attacker used a credential stolen from Fireworks AI to publish two tampered copies of Anthropic's Claude Code extension (2.1.312 and 2.1.313) on October 29; they were live about 11 hours and up to 5,000 developers may have downloaded them.
💡 Why it matters: The payload collected system information, installed an attacker-controlled SSH key and set up remote access — and the backdoor persisted past extension removal via LaunchAgents on macOS, cron on Linux and scheduled tasks on Windows. Exposed coding-agent traces at Fireworks also contained unredacted API credentials. It is a complete cross-vendor lateral movement case — model vendor to inference provider to extension marketplace — and shows how thin the trust boundary under agent tooling really is.

**6. The FAA grounded all US flights four hours, three days after its SMART AI tool went nationwide**
🏷️ Source: AI Weekly / Futurism (with Scientific American, AVweb reporting)
📝 In one line: The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop on Friday lasting about four hours, with more than 3,500 aircraft airborne and 827 flights diverted — the first full US airspace halt since January 2023.
💡 Why it matters: It came three days after SMART (Strategic Management of Airspace Routes and Trajectories) was expanded to all 22 regional air traffic control centres. The Transportation Secretary blamed a communications issue and overloaded routers; DHS said it was "looking at" SMART; Air Space Intelligence, which won a 12-year, $875M contract in June, denied any link. Officials have neither confirmed nor cleared the system — and either way the incident puts AI-in-critical-infrastructure accountability and incident reporting squarely on the table.

### Today's takeaway

Google industrialized the production of machine-checked proofs, Meituan shipped a trillion-scale model together with a domestic-chip deployment plan, and LangChain was repriced at $3B — each is a sign that models and agents are becoming someone else's utility. The week's three safety stories are the risk shapes that only appear after that happens: models that can edit each other's weights, an extension marketplace broken open by a stolen supply-chain key, and national airspace management halting three days after its AI tool went live. Governance is moving too. Both the credibility of evaluations and the latency of incident disclosure are now being demanded by markets and legislators at once.
