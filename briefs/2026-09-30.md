**1. 📌 Anthropic's Frontier Red Team: GLM-5.3 builds end-to-end cyberattacks, and its safeguards are trivially bypassed**
- 🏷️ Source: Anthropic Frontier Red Team / Simon Willison ([link](https://www.anthropic.com/research/glm-5-3-and-the-spread-of-advanced-cyber-capabilities)) (2026-09-29)
- 📝 In a sentence: Anthropic's Frontier Red Team analysed GLM-5.3, the open-weight model from Zhipu AI (Z.ai), and found its autonomous exploit-building ability has caught up with its own restricted Claude Mythos Preview: on an internal binary-exploitation benchmark GLM-5.3 achieved full control-flow hijacks in 4% of trials (Mythos Preview 6%), while Claude Opus 4.6 and GLM-5.2 scored zero; on ExploitBench the two were close (50 of 410 attempts versus 56). In one session, a researcher spent a day and under an hour of human attention to chain several zero-days the model found in a browser's JavaScript engine into a web page that steals a visitor's SSH private key. NIST's CAISI separately called GLM-5.3 "the most cyber-capable open-weight model released to date", about four months behind the US frontier.
- 💡 Why it matters: This turns "capability proliferation" from an abstract warning into numbers. Frontier-level offensive cyber capability now sits in a model anyone can download and strip of its safeguards, while Anthropic's equivalent model is released only to vetted defenders. When capability and safeguards are separated, the safety gap that voluntary controls were meant to hold formally closes — and governments may have to regulate weights and compute, not just APIs.
